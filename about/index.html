Spoiler alert: I've moved… a lot.

Dallas born and raised, then Montana, Colorado, and eleven years in Washington before Texas called me home. So I know exactly what it feels like to have a job lined up, a move date creeping closer, and absolutely no idea which part of town fits your life.

How this started

Everybody has an opinion. Very few have a map.

Relocating comes with a lot of opinions. Your cousin. TikTok. A guy at work who bought a house in 2019 and is suddenly a real estate expert.

What it rarely comes with is a straight answer to the question that actually matters: which of these places fits the way you live? Not the way your coworker lives. Not the version of you that looks good on paper. You.

So I built myDFWmatch to start with your answers instead of everybody else's opinions. It runs what you tell me about the commute, the house, and the everyday stuff against real rush-hour drive times, Census housing data, and actual maps of what's nearby. Then it shows you where I'd start looking, why, and what, if anything, you'd be giving up to get it.

It's the tool I wish I'd had long before I packed up the moving truck.

What I believe

A technically “great” area doesn't matter much if it doesn't fit the way your average Tuesday actually goes.

The tradeoffs stay visible.

Every area asks you to give something up. You should know what it is before you fall in love with a listing.

Everything is figureoutable.

New city, new job, new everything. You bring me where you are, and we'll figure out how to get where you want to go.

What this is (and isn't)

A useful starting point.

Broad search areas, built around how you live, with the tradeoffs left right where you can see them.

Not a “best neighborhoods” list.

It doesn't rate schools, crime, safety, or the people who live anywhere. It describes geography, housing, and amenities. You decide what fits.

Not a crystal ball.

The exact address, today's listings, and your real commute still get the final word. Here's how the match works.
